CONTENT:

Banco de España identifies housing-supply constraints as an important
factor underlying Spain's current housing affordability problems.

Housing supply has responded insufficiently to rising demand over the
past decade, contributing to a persistent mismatch between housing
demand and supply and sustained increases in house prices.

The supply situation varies significantly between regions.

Housing availability is particularly constrained in some areas where
economic activity and population growth are concentrated.

Banco de España identifies several factors affecting housing supply,
including development-land availability, planning and administrative
processes, construction-sector capacity, productivity, profitability,
production costs and shortages of specialised labour.

REGULATORY CONTEXT:

Spain's Law 12/2023 allows competent authorities to designate
stressed residential market areas where there is evidence of
insufficient affordable housing supply.

The legislation provides for specific measures affecting rental
markets in designated stressed areas.

Depending on the circumstances, restrictions may affect the rent
that can be charged under certain new rental contracts.

Additional provisions can apply to large residential landlords.

Because housing regulation and stressed-market designations can vary
geographically, national Spanish housing statistics alone are not
sufficient for assessing an individual investment.

INVESTMENT INTERPRETATION:

Supply constraints combined with strong demand may support house
prices and rental demand.

However, investors must consider regional variation and local
regulation.

A national Spanish market indicator should therefore be used as a
screening signal rather than evidence that a particular city,
neighbourhood or property is attractive.

Local rental regulation, transaction costs, taxation, financing,
achievable rent and property-specific characteristics would need to
be assessed separately.
