CONTENT:

Ireland continues to face significant housing-supply requirements.

The Government's Delivering Homes, Building Communities 2025-2030
plan targets the delivery of 300,000 homes by the end of 2030.

The policy response includes measures intended to activate land,
increase infrastructure provision, improve construction viability,
support investment, increase construction skills and address vacancy
and dereliction.

The Central Bank of Ireland expects housing output to increase over
the coming years and projects approximately 45,000 units in 2028.

However, the Central Bank has also identified constraints affecting
housing delivery.

Planning-to-completion times have increased significantly since 2017,
and construction capacity and infrastructure availability remain
important factors affecting the ability of housing supply to respond
to demand.

INVESTMENT INTERPRETATION:

Persistent supply constraints can support property prices and rents
when housing demand remains strong.

However, strong price growth caused partly by constrained supply
should not automatically be interpreted as an attractive investment
signal.

Investors should also consider affordability, financing conditions,
future housing delivery, regulation, location and achievable rental
income.

National-level indicators cannot establish the investment quality of
an individual Irish property.
